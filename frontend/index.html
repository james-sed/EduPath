How EduPath works.

Four stops between you and a clear plan. No guesswork, and every result explains itself.

  1. Take the skill assessment

    Rate your technical skills in programming, databases, testing, and systems tools. It takes about five minutes.

  2. See your role matches

    Machine learning models rank six roles by how well your results fit each one, and show which skills decided it.

  3. Get your skill gap analysis

    See your match percentage and exactly how many points you are short on each skill the role needs.

  4. Follow your learning pathway

    Every skill gap maps to JRU CSE subjects and CHED-recognized microcredentials.

Six roles, one assessment.

You're scored against every role, so you see your best fit and your next-best options.

    Questions, answered.

    Is EduPath a job board?

    No. EduPath doesn't list jobs, take applications, or connect you with employers. It shows which roles fit your skills today and what to learn next.

    Does a high match mean I'll get hired?

    No. Role matches and learning pathways are guidance for planning your studies. They are not job offers or placement guarantees.

    Where do the role benchmarks come from?

    From industry frameworks for entry-level IT roles. JRU subjects and CHED-recognized microcredentials are the catalog EduPath recommends from, not the yardstick it measures you against.

    What happens to my data?

    EduPath is built to follow the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173) and keeps only what your results need.

    Is this an official JRU service?

    No. EduPath is an independent student project by TypeShift, built for students of José Rizal University's College of Computer Studies and Engineering.
